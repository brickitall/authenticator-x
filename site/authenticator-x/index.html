Built to be trusted, not just used

An authenticator holds the second half of every login you protect. So it is built to know as little as possible, and to show its work.

Encrypted on your computer

Your accounts are sealed with AES-256-GCM under a key only you hold. Nothing readable is ever written to disk.

No access to websites

It asks for no permission to read or change the sites you visit. Scanning a QR code or filling in a code works only on the tab you open it on, at the moment you open it.

Open source

The extension and its encryption are published under GPL-3.0. Read it, build it yourself, and compare your build with the one the store ships.

Nothing phones home

No analytics, no ads, no tracking. Service logos are built in, so no website ever learns which accounts you keep.

Everything you need to sign in

Works with any site that offers an authenticator app — the standard six-digit codes.

  • Scan the QR code straight from the page you are setting up
  • Or point your camera at a code shown on your phone
  • Move everything over from Google Authenticator
  • Fill a code into the page with one click
  • Groups and search, for when the list gets long
  • Locks itself after the time you choose
  • Encrypted backups you can restore anywhere
  • Standard otpauth:// export, so you can leave whenever you like
Coming next

Sync, if you want it. An optional account will keep your codes in step across browsers, end-to-end encrypted: the server stores only what it cannot read, and the code that encrypts it is the part that is open source.

No account will ever be required. Without one, nothing changes.

No extension at hand?

Paste a setup key into the code page and get the current code. It is worked out in that page, never sent anywhere and never kept — and the page's own security policy, enforced by your browser, is what makes that so.
